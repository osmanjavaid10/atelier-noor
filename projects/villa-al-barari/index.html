Villa Al Barari

The east pavilion and pool in the early morning.

A family house of deep shade and long water, planned so every room looks into a garden and none looks into the sun.

The brief

A family of six wanted a house they could live in for eight months of the year without drawing the curtains. Their previous villa was glass on every side and, in their words, 'a greenhouse with sofas'.

The site is long and narrow, running west to east between two mature groves of ghaf trees that had to stay.

Our approach

We turned the house on its side. Instead of one block facing the street, the plan is a sequence of three pavilions linked by shaded loggias, each opening north onto its own garden. The south faces are almost solid: 600 mm walls of rendered blockwork with small, deep openings.

A 32-metre pool runs the length of the site between the pavilions and the trees. It cools the air that the house draws through its loggias in the evening, and it gives the children a straight line to swim.

Roofs overhang by 2.4 metres. In June, no direct sun touches the glazing after 9:40 in the morning.
